# 🏆 Pokémon Triple Battle Rulebook

Triple Battles are a unique and highly strategic battle format introduced in Generation V (*Pokémon Black and White*) and present through Generation VI. In this format, each trainer sends out **three Pokémon at once**. 

Unlike standard Double Battles, Triple Battles introduce a spatial mechanic: **Positioning** dictates what your Pokémon can and cannot do. 

---

## 1. 📍 The Battlefield Layout

The battlefield consists of six active slots, three on each side. The positioning of your Pokémon—Left, Center, or Right—is the most important factor in a Triple Battle.

**Your Side:** `[ Left (A) ]` `[ Center (B) ]` `[ Right (C) ]`
**Opponent:** `[ Right (Z) ]` `[ Center (Y) ]` `[ Left (X) ]`

*Note: From your perspective, your opponent's "Left" Pokémon is directly across from your "Right" Pokémon.*

---

## 2. 🎯 Range and Targeting Restrictions

In a Single or Double battle, any Pokémon can target any opponent. In a Triple Battle, attacks have **limited range**. A Pokémon can only hit targets that are "adjacent" to it.

### The Edge Positions (Left & Right)
Pokémon on the far left or far right have limited reach. 
* **Your Left Pokémon** can target: Your Center, Opponent's Center, Opponent's Right.
* **Your Left Pokémon CANNOT target:** Your Right, Opponent's Left.
* The exact same mirror rules apply to the Right position.

### The Center Position
The Center slot is the most powerful and most dangerous position on the field.
* **Your Center Pokémon** can target **ANY** Pokémon on the field. 
* However, your Center Pokémon can also be targeted by **EVERY** Pokémon on the opponent's field. 

---

## 3. 🔄 The "Shift" Command

Because edge Pokémon cannot reach the opposite side of the field, the game introduces a new action: **Shifting**.

* Instead of using a move, a Pokémon in the Left or Right position can use its turn to **Shift**.
* Shifting swaps that Pokémon's position with the Center Pokémon. 
* This action occurs at the **Priority 0** bracket, meaning it resolves based on the Shifting Pokémon's Speed stat relative to other attackers. 
* If a Pokémon uses an attack aimed at a slot, and the target Shifts away, the attack will hit the new Pokémon occupying that slot instead.

---

## 4. 🌊 Spread Moves

Moves that hit multiple targets behave differently depending on who uses them and where they are placed.

* **Used by Edge Pokémon:** If your Left Pokémon uses *Earthquake* (hits all adjacent) or *Heat Wave* (hits all adjacent opponents), it will only hit the Pokémon in its range. It will miss the Pokémon on the far opposite side.
* **Used by Center Pokémon:** If the Center Pokémon uses a spread move, it hits **all valid targets on the entire field**. A Center *Earthquake* will hit all 5 other Pokémon simultaneously.

*Damage Reduction:* Just like in Double battles, spread moves suffer a **25% damage reduction** when hitting multiple targets.

---

## 5. 🏹 Long-Range Moves (Exceptions to the Rule)

Certain moves bypass the spatial restrictions of Triple Battles entirely. These are known as **Long-Range Moves** and can hit *any* Pokémon on the field, regardless of the user's position.

If a Left-positioned Pokémon uses one of these moves, it CAN hit the far opposite side of the field.

**Types of Long-Range Moves:**
1. **Flying-type standard attacks:** E.g., *Acrobatics, Brave Bird, Hurricane, Roost, Pluck, Peck, Drill Peck, Fly, Bounce*.
2. **Pulse / Aura moves:** E.g., *Aura Sphere, Dark Pulse, Water Pulse, Dragon Pulse, Heal Pulse*.
3. **Specific Status Moves:** E.g., *Perish Song* (affects everyone regardless of position).

---

## 6. 💀 Fainting and Replacements

* If a Pokémon faints, it is replaced at the **end of the turn**, exactly like in Double Battles.
* If multiple Pokémon faint on the same turn, the trainer chooses which replacement goes into which empty slot.
* If a Pokémon was targeting a slot, and the Pokémon in that slot faints before the attack hits, the attack will **NOT automatically redirect** if it involves crossing out of range. If the only remaining opponent is out of range, the attack will simply fail.

---

## 7. ⏱️ Turn Order Sequence

1. **Check Move Priority Brackets**: Priority always wins, regardless of Speed. The game first groups all six Pokémon by the priority bracket of the move they selected. A higher number always goes first. For example, Protect is +4, Fake Out is +3, Follow Me is +2, standard attacks are 0, and Trick Room is -7.
2. **Compare Speed Stats**: If two or more Pokémon use moves in the same priority bracket (e.g., both use Priority 0 attacks), the game compares their current Speed stats. The higher Speed goes first. If Trick Room is active, this step is reversed, and the lower Speed goes first. However, Trick Room never overrides Step 1 (Priority).
3. **Resolve Speed Ties**: If Pokémon are in the same priority bracket and have the exact same Speed stat, the game literally flips a virtual coin. It is a 50/50 chance who goes first between those tied Pokémon.
4. **Apply Dynamic Recalculation**: Generation 8 onwards. If a Pokémon's Speed changes mid-turn (for example, if it gets paralyzed by a faster opponent's Thunder Wave), the turn order instantly recalculates for the remaining actions. If a Pokémon is now slower than the remaining attackers, it will be bumped down the queue.

---

## ⚖️ Summary of Strategy
Winning a Triple Battle requires careful management of the **Center slot**. You must place your bulky setup Pokémon or powerful spread attackers in the center to maximize their reach, while using the edge slots to protect fragile attackers or utilize long-range moves. Shifting should be used to rescue Pokémon in danger or set up unexpected sweeps.
